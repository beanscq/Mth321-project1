\section{Numerical Methods}
Explicit Euler, classical RK4, and implicit Euler are implemented directly for the controlled numerical experiments. SciPy's \texttt{solve\_ivp} interface is used separately for reference-solution validation and library-solver comparisons~\cite{scipy}. This separation keeps the custom-method experiments under direct control while allowing independent comparisons with established library solvers.

\subsection{Explicit Euler}
Explicit Euler is $y_{n+1}=y_n+h f(t_n,y_n)$. Taylor expansion gives local truncation error $O(h^2)$ and global error $O(h)$. It uses one right-hand-side evaluation per step. For $y'=\lambda y$,
\[
R_{EE}(z)=1+z,\qquad z=h\lambda,
\]
and the negative-real stability interval is $[-2,0]$.
Explicit Euler is stable on the negative real axis only for $-2\leq z\leq0$, because $|1+z|\leq1$ on that interval. A sufficiently large negative value of $h\lambda$ therefore leaves the stability region even when the concentration appears slowly varying. For Robertson, this bound applies to the locally linearised Jacobian modes.

\subsection{Classical RK4}
RK4 is
\begin{align*}
k_1&=f(t_n,y_n),&k_2&=f(t_n+h/2,y_n+hk_1/2),\\
k_3&=f(t_n+h/2,y_n+hk_2/2),&k_4&=f(t_n+h,y_n+hk_3),\\
y_{n+1}&=y_n+\frac h6(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4).
\end{align*}
For smooth $f$, local and global errors are $O(h^5)$ and $O(h^4)$; each step costs four right-hand-side evaluations. Applying the stages to the scalar test equation gives
\[
R_{RK4}(z)=1+z+\frac{z^2}{2}+\frac{z^3}{6}+\frac{z^4}{24}.
\]
RK4 remains subject to a bounded absolute-stability region despite its fourth-order accuracy. On the negative real axis, $|R_{RK4}(z)|\leq1$ gives a left endpoint of approximately $z=-2.7853$ and the interval $[-2.7853,0]$. Robertson's rapidly decaying mode can therefore restrict $h$ even when RK4 resolves the slower components accurately.

\subsection{Implicit Euler and Newton iteration}
Implicit Euler satisfies
\[
y_{n+1}=y_n+h f(t_{n+1},y_{n+1}),
\]
and is first order. Defining $G(u)=u-y_n-hf(t_{n+1},u)$, Newton computes
\begin{align*}
[I-hJ_f(t_{n+1},u^{(k)})]\Delta u^{(k)}&=-G(u^{(k)}),\\
u^{(k+1)}&=u^{(k)}+\Delta u^{(k)}.
\end{align*}
The stability function $R_{IE}(z)=1/(1-z)$ contains the left half-plane, so implicit Euler is A-stable for the linear test equation. This stability property does not control large-step accuracy, which also depends on the nonlinear solve. In our implementation, Newton iteration starts from the explicit-Euler predictor $u^{(0)}=y_n+hf(t_n,y_n)$, which reuses the derivative already stored at $t_n$ and therefore costs no extra evaluation. It uses the infinity norm of the nonlinear residual and permits at most 50 iterations. The standard uniform-step experiments use a residual tolerance of $10^{-12}$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Implicit Euler with Newton iteration}
\label{alg:implicit-euler-newton}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $y_n$, $t_n$, step size $h$, residual tolerance $\mathrm{tol}_N$, maximum iterations $k_{\max}$
\Ensure accepted $y_{n+1}$ or a Newton-failure status
\State $u^{(0)}\gets y_n+h f(t_n,y_n)$ \Comment{explicit-Euler predictor}
\For{$k=0,1,\ldots,k_{\max}$}
  \State $G^{(k)}\gets u^{(k)}-y_n-hf(t_n+h,u^{(k)})$
  \If{$\|G^{(k)}\|_\infty\leq \mathrm{tol}_N$}
    \State \Return $y_{n+1}\gets u^{(k)}$
  \EndIf
  \If{$k=k_{\max}$}
    \State \Return Newton-failure status
  \EndIf
  \State $DG^{(k)}\gets I-hJ_f(t_n+h,u^{(k)})$
  \State Solve $DG^{(k)}\Delta u^{(k)}=-G^{(k)}$
  \State $u^{(k+1)}\gets u^{(k)}+\Delta u^{(k)}$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:implicit-euler-newton} gives the Newton iteration used for each implicit Euler step. It starts from the explicit-Euler predictor and stops when the infinity norm of the nonlinear residual satisfies $\mathrm{tol}_N$. Our uniform-step runs use $k_{\max}=50$ and $\mathrm{tol}_N=10^{-12}$.

The residual, rather than the Newton correction, is the right quantity to stop on for this problem: since $(1,1,1)^Tf\equiv0$, one step changes the invariant by exactly $(1,1,1)^TG(z)=\sum_iG_i(z)$, so $\|G\|_\infty$ bounds the invariant defect the step can introduce, while a tolerance on $\|\Delta u\|$ does not. The production runs use plain Newton without damping. A damped variant (backtracking on $\alpha$, halving from $\alpha=1$ with a strict-decrease test) is implemented for robustness but was not needed: across all reported uniform-step, sweep, and Newton-sweep runs there are no failed Newton steps. If a solve does fail, the run records the failure (non-finite residual, singular linear solve, stagnation, or iteration limit), recomputes the stored derivative so that the dense output belongs to the returned state, and continues; in fixed-step mode $h$ is not cut.
