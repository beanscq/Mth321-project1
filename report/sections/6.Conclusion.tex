\section{Conclusions}
\paragraph{1. Conservation alone does not certify accuracy.} The invariant can remain near machine precision while the trajectory becomes negative and inaccurate. The explicit-Euler counterexample therefore requires conservation, non-negativity, full-state error, and endpoint error to be assessed separately.

\paragraph{2. Frozen-Jacobian analysis predicts the scale of explicit instability.} The estimate is local and the empirical sweep brackets the nonlinear transition. For EE, the $5.895\times10^{-4}$ estimate differs by about eight percent from the $5.4159\times10^{-4}$ largest stable tested step.

\paragraph{3. Greater stability did not reduce matched-accuracy cost in the nominal model.} First-order IE requires more work than EE at every tested target reached by both methods. This result uses $C=N_f+N_J+N_{\mathrm{solve}}$ and remains subject to final verification of the operation counters.

\paragraph{4. Solver tolerances change the realised numerical behaviour.} A loose Newton threshold can accept the explicit predictor, while an unscaled adaptive norm can admit physically invalid steps. Across the tested adaptive settings, mixed control completes all four runs without negative concentrations. Absolute $L^\infty$ control produces one inaccurate negative solution and one early termination. The accepted-state formula must still be checked in our final implementation.
