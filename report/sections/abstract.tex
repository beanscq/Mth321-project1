\begin{abstract}
The Robertson problem is a three-species stiff chemical-kinetics initial-value problem. We compare explicit Euler (EE), classical fourth-order Runge--Kutta (RK4), and implicit Euler (IE) solved by Newton iteration. Along a tight reference trajectory, the two nonzero Jacobian modes separate to a stiffness ratio of $1.5840\times10^5$ at $t=40$, with fast-mode magnitude $3392.79$. Frozen-Jacobian analysis therefore predicts $h_{EE}\lesssim5.895\times10^{-4}$ and $h_{RK4}\lesssim8.209\times10^{-4}$; these are local diagnostics, not nonlinear stability theorems. The approved presentation reports measured orders $1.079$, $4.268$, and $0.99996$, largest stable tested steps $5.4159\times10^{-4}$ and $8.3088\times10^{-4}$, and $y_2(40)=9.185535\times10^{-6}$. We show why conservation cannot certify accuracy, compare work at matched error, and analyse Newton and adaptive tolerances. Original scientific figures replace slide screenshots. \end{abstract}
